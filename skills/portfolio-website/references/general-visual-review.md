# Visual direction and review for the actual brief

[简体中文](zh-CN/general-visual-review.md)

This guide applies across website and product types. A convincing result makes the intended task understandable, expresses the right character for its audience, and is executed carefully. There is no single palette, typeface, layout, amount of whitespace, density, or motion style that makes every interface feel premium.

## Decide what this project needs

1. Write down the person, their immediate goal, what they must understand or decide, and the next real action. For an existing product, inspect the current flow and established design system first.
2. Use the supplied brand, content, accessibility needs, language, devices, technical constraints and the user's explicit preferences. Treat assumptions as assumptions. Ask about a consequential gap when it cannot be resolved from context; otherwise proceed with a stated, revisable direction.
3. Describe the intended character in terms that can affect choices: for example, reassuring and precise, warm and energetic, or restrained and editorial. Explain how that character serves this audience and task. A serious financial tool and a lively financial education site can both be well designed; an industry label alone does not decide the look.
4. Choose information hierarchy, navigation, density, type, imagery, colour roles, surfaces, controls and motion together. Explain the main tradeoff. Consider the actual content, states and devices, not a decorative mood board alone.
5. If the brief is open or the choice materially affects the result, compare credible directions and select or present the appropriate one. If the user has already chosen a direction or an existing system applies, honor it; do not introduce artificial variation. Related products can deliberately share a system.

Examples are prompts for reasoning, not style assignments: the same appointment service could use a calm, explicit step-by-step flow for an anxious first-time visitor, or a compact, high-density schedule for an experienced coordinator. The distinction affects the task and interaction as well as visual expression. Conversely, two unrelated brands may both need a similar table when comparison is the job.

## Review the rendered result

Review at the relevant desktop and phone widths using real content where permitted. Record the browser, tested sizes, actions and any unverified areas. A screenshot or automated check cannot establish visual quality by itself.

| Question | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Does the page fit the purpose and person? | A reader can identify the offer or current task, understand what matters, and find the next action without inventing credibility or content. |
| Is the intended character coherent? | Typography, imagery, colour, density, layout and interaction support the same brief. Existing brand constraints and deliberate exceptions are respected. |
| Is the hierarchy useful? | Headings, groupings and emphasis support the task. Dense comparison may have several important regions; a campaign can use a single dominant image. Neither is mandatory. |
| Is the craft resolved? | Check real font rendering and language coverage, line breaks, alignment, spacing, image crops, icon weight, borders and surface edges. Look for accidental inconsistency, clipping, missing content and poor contrast. Whitespace must serve grouping and reading; more is not automatically better. |
| Can people operate it? | Exercise the real primary path, navigation and relevant empty, error, recovery and changed-input states; inspect focus, keyboard, touch, responsive behavior and reduced motion as applicable. |
| Does motion earn its cost? | Changes remain understandable without animation; timing, performance and any visual effect suit the context. Static feedback can be the right choice. |

Review with actual long and short copy, mixed scripts and realistic data. Inspect intermediate widths where the composition changes, not just a device preset. Explain a visual criticism in observable terms and tie it to the brief; avoid declaring a page good or bad because it lacks a prescribed hero, shadow, radius, heading length or decorative effect.

## Interpret the legacy checks

The older portfolio case studies and executable self-check contain useful observations and portfolio-derived heuristics. They are optional instruments, not a universal taste score. A tool finding is a reason to inspect: fix a confirmed accessibility, functional or rendering problem, but explain a rule that does not fit this project rather than forcing the design to satisfy it. A clean report does not prove the visual direction is right, and an unmeasured state is not a pass. Keep the tool's exit codes and evidence intact when reporting its result.
